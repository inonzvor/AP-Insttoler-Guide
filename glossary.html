Glossary

The terms in this manual, in plain language, for someone new to Wi‑Fi access points or to Hikvision gear.

20 Key Terms A–Z Reference Aug. 2026 Reference

Install this app

  1. Tap the Share icon in Safari's toolbar.
  2. Scroll down and choose Add to Home Screen.
Access point (AP)
A device that adds Wi‑Fi to a wired network. It does not route or share an internet connection — a router upstream does that. All DS‑3WAP models are access points.
Activation
The one‑time first step on a new Hikvision device: it ships with no password and does nothing until you set a strong admin password. Done in SADP, the web page, or Hik‑Partner Pro.
AC (wireless controller)
"Access Controller" — hardware (or a software function) that manages many APs as one system: one config pushed to all, coordinated channels, seamless roaming. See FAT / FIT.
Band steering
An AP feature that nudges dual‑band clients onto the less crowded 5 GHz band instead of 2.4 GHz, when they can support it.
Channel / channel width
The slice of radio spectrum a band uses. Wider channels (40/80 MHz) are faster but overlap neighbours more. Rule of thumb: 20 MHz on 2.4 GHz, 80 MHz on 5 GHz.
DFS (Dynamic Frequency Selection)
Rules that let Wi‑Fi use extra 5 GHz channels shared with radar. The AP must vacate a channel if it detects radar, so a client may briefly drop on those channels.
DHCP
The service (usually on the router) that hands out IP addresses automatically. A DS‑3WAP is normally a DHCP client — it receives an address, it does not give them out.
FAT / FIT mode
FAT (a.k.a. standalone) = the AP holds its own config and you set it up on the AP itself. FIT = the AP is managed by a controller (AC), which holds the config. Adopting an AP to a controller switches it FAT → FIT.
Hik‑Connect
The end‑customer app/account for viewing and managing Hikvision devices. At handover you transfer the site to the customer's Hik‑Connect.
Hik‑Partner Pro
Hikvision's cloud platform and app for installers/partners (formerly Hik‑ProConnect): add customer sites, activate and configure devices remotely, get health alerts, then hand the site to the customer.
HikCentral Professional
Hikvision's on‑premise enterprise management software for large, multi‑site systems — cameras, access control, alarms, and APs together.
IP rating (IP65 / IP68)
Sealing against dust and water. IP65 = dust‑tight, resists water jets (general outdoor). IP68 = also survives prolonged immersion (harsh/exposed sites).
MAC address
A device's permanent hardware identifier, printed on its label. Use it to pick the right unit out of a list of identical models in SADP or the router.
MU‑MIMO / OFDMA
Techniques that let an AP serve several clients in the same airtime instead of one at a time. OFDMA (Wi‑Fi 6) is the bigger win in crowded rooms.
PoE (Power over Ethernet)
Power and data on one network cable, so an AP needs no separate mains supply at the mounting point. Standards: 802.3af (~13 W), 802.3at / PoE+ (~25 W).
PoE injector
A small mains‑powered box that adds PoE to one cable run, for when the site switch is not PoE. Sits between the switch and the AP; keep it indoors.
Roaming
A client moving from one AP to the next without dropping its connection. Needs overlapping coverage and, ideally, a controller coordinating the APs.
SADP (Search Active Devices Protocol)
Hikvision's free Windows/macOS tool that finds Hikvision devices on the local network, shows their IP/MAC/status, and can activate them and change their IP.
SSID
The Wi‑Fi network name clients see and join. One AP can broadcast several (e.g. staff + guest), each with its own security and VLAN.
Uplink
The wired port (and cable) that connects the AP back to the switch. Its speed — 1 GbE, or 2.5 GbE on the 622E‑SI — caps the AP's total throughput to the wired side.
VLAN
A way to run separate logical networks over one physical switch. An SSID can be tagged to a VLAN so, e.g., guest Wi‑Fi is isolated from the office network.
WPA2 / WPA3
Wi‑Fi security standards. WPA2 is universally supported; WPA3 is stronger but needs modern clients. "WPA2/WPA3‑mixed" accepts both — the safe default.

Specifications compiled from official Hikvision datasheets and product pages (hikvision.com, assets.hikvision.com), current as of August 2026.
